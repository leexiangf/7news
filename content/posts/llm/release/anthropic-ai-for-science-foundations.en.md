On 28 October 2026 Anthropic said it is expanding AI for Science. The story is no longer only “credits for researchers.” The company is naming partnerships with about 20 scientific foundations, institutions, and labs. The offer remains free Claude credits for high-impact research projects.

Anthropic dates the program to May 2025. In recent months it also launched a discounted Team plan for scientists and says it has backed hundreds of projects across biology, medicine, physics, chemistry, climate, and math. “Hundreds” is the company’s word. Researchers, as Anthropic recounts them, use Claude to propose hypotheses, design experiments, analyze complex data, and draft findings—scientists set the questions; the model speeds the loop.

The more distinctive thread is how the collaborations are shaped. With HHMI Janelia, Anthropic is pushing a Model Hardware Standard (MHS) so AI agents can operate lab instruments under tighter controls; the company marks it as a research preview. Work with the Allen Institute points at open brain, cell, and immune datasets, with agents aimed at turning months of manual analysis into hours. Other partners are not expanded here; use the official page as the list of record.

For research engineering and lab-automation teams, split the signal into three lanes: free or discounted compute, agent analysis on shared datasets, and instrument-side interface specs. The first two change how cheaply you can run; MHS changes whether it is safe to touch hardware. How open MHS becomes, and who can apply for credits, is still bounded by the announcement itself. If you only track token policy, you may miss the layer that actually changes wet-lab workflows.
