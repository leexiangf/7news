Google DeepMind said on 10 November 2026 that Gemini 4 Carbon is available to developers, enterprises, scientists, and consumers. The post positions Carbon as the heavier sibling of Gemini 4 Argon: stronger on long-horizon agentic workflows, complex knowledge work, software engineering, and scientific reasoning—after an earlier Fairwind preview for cybersecurity defenders.

DeepMind is explicit about the split. Argon stays the practical default for most engineering and knowledge tasks. Carbon is for work that stresses model limits—multi-step coding and debugging, open-ended research—accepting higher token use for thorough exploration, deeper reasoning, and extended verification. The company cites top ranks on several workflow-oriented external benches (CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity’s WANDR, Zapier’s AutomationBench, among others) with claimed cost-per-task advantages. Treat those as vendor-reported; cross-model comparisons still need aligned suites and billing definitions.

Access is role-split: paid developers via the Gemini API in Google AI Studio; enterprises via Gemini for business; Google AI Ultra subscribers get rollout in Google Antigravity, the Gemini app, and AI Mode in Search. Antigravity previews `/swarm`, turning one prompt into a coordinated agent team. Carbon can take the deepest roles (architecture, coordination, audit) while lighter models run in parallel workspaces. Cyber defenders can still apply to Fairwind; the scientist program that offered Argon for six months now includes Carbon, with rolling onboarding subject to eligibility and capacity.

Pricing: introductory rates match Argon at $2 / $10 per million input / output tokens through **30 November 2026**. After that, public Carbon is listed at $4 / $20. The post also claims stronger misuse and prompt-injection mitigations versus Argon and points to a separate Safety and Security report—use that document and third-party retests for the detailed numbers.

If Argon already holds your production path, do not switch for novelty. Pilot Carbon where long-horizon agents, dense multimodal knowledge work, or defensive cyber research actually need deeper loops—and watch the post-intro bill, whether `/swarm` enters daily practice, and how Fast Fairwind or research seats actually clear.
