On 6 November 2026 OpenAI announced a partnership with Hacktron to find vulnerabilities in OpenAI’s own systems. Engineers from both sides will pick high-priority targets together. The post singles out one theme: how OpenAI systems interact with third-party services—not only single-product surface bugs.

OpenAI says it first met Hacktron through the Security Bug Bounty program and was impressed by the team’s disclosures. Hacktron builds AI agents for code review, penetration testing, and red teaming; its public research often sits at the seams between applications, cloud infrastructure, and identity. That matches OpenAI’s own framing: as AI agents wire more services together, those seams get more valuable to attack—and to defend.

The same week’s context matters. OpenAI also said Cathedral researchers will join the next security sprint and raised bug-bounty rewards. The Hacktron deal is a named partnership inside that broader “find our own holes faster” push, not a model launch. As part of it, Hacktron gets expanded access to frontier models through Daybreak Red.

For security-minded readers, three takeaways: third-party boundaries are an official priority; an independent red team is invited into joint scoping instead of only the public bounty queue; and frontier cyber models via Daybreak Red are treated as a research accelerator for the partner. Scope details and bounty rules still live on the Bug Bounty page. The announcement does not publish a timeline or a quota by vulnerability class.
