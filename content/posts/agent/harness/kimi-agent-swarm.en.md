Moonshot (Kimi) introduces **Agent Swarm**: not “one stronger assistant,” but a runtime-self-organizing team—coordination, retrieval, analysis, and fact-check hired on the spot rather than a hand-written if-else tree.

## Why single agents hit a wall

Long-horizon serial runs fill the context window; systems then fold or summarize to make room—lossy compression degrades later reasoning. The post frames this as a structural ceiling of windows, time, and single-point management. The answer is **horizontal scale**: parallel sub-agent networks for broad discovery, batch downloads, multi-file work, and multi-angle writing—plus “independent conclusions, then forced reconciliation” so disagreement is a feature.

## Checkable numbers and use cases

With **Kimi K2.5**:

| Claim | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Parallel sub-agents | Up to ~**100** |
| Tool calls | **1,500+** |
| Vs serial | ~**4.5×** faster |

Fits: wide retrieval (e.g. top creators across 100 niche YouTube domains), scattered essay corpora, ~100-page lit reviews from dozens of PDFs, multi-style continuations—shareable demo links in the post. Speed/quality comparisons are vendor-stated; gains depend on true parallelizability and whether synthesis introduces new failure modes.

## Availability

**Top-tier subscribers**, labeled **early research preview**. Roadmap hardening: direct sub-agent communication, dynamic parallel width. Strict serial state machines should not assume 100-way parallelism is safer. Watch preview entitlement, failure/conflict reporting, and when comms/width controls land.
